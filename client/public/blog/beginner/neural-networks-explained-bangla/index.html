What is a Neural Network?

A neural network is a computer system designed to recognize patterns, inspired by how the human brain works. Just as your brain has billions of neurons connected to each other, a neural network has thousands of artificial "neurons" organized in layers.

The Analogy: A Team of Experts

Imagine you're trying to identify a photo of a cat, but you have a team of specialists each looking at one small detail:

  • Layer 1 (Input): One person looks at edges â€” straight lines, curves, angles
  • Layer 2 (Hidden): Another person combines edges into shapes â€” circles, triangles, rectangles
  • Layer 3 (Hidden): Another person combines shapes into features â€” eyes, ears, nose, whiskers
  • Layer 4 (Output): The final person says "These features together = a cat!"

Each "person" is a layer of neurons. The more layers, the "deeper" the network, and the more complex patterns it can recognize.

How Neural Networks Learn

  1. Forward pass: Data enters the network and flows through all layers, generating an output
  2. Error calculation: The network compares its output to the correct answer
  3. Backpropagation: The error is sent backward through the network, adjusting the connections between neurons
  4. Iteration: Steps 1-3 repeat thousands or millions of times until the network becomes accurate
